# Cloud Computing Fundamentals

## 1. What is cloud computing?

Cloud computing is the delivery of computing services—such as servers, storage, databases, networking, and software—over a network, usually the internet. Instead of buying and maintaining all the physical equipment yourself, you can use resources provided by a cloud provider.

**Simple example:** Instead of storing every file on a physical hard drive, you can use Google Drive to store and access files online. For computing, a company can rent a virtual server instead of buying a physical server.

> A cloud is not just one server. It is a collection of technology and services that a provider makes available to customers.

## 2. Why do organizations use cloud computing?

Traditional IT infrastructure can require an organization to buy servers, provide electricity and cooling, maintain equipment, and plan for hardware failures or disasters.

Cloud services can help organizations:
- Obtain computing resources when needed.
- Scale resources up or down as demand changes.
- Reduce the need to purchase physical hardware upfront.
- Access services over a network.
- Use provider-managed infrastructure services.

Cloud does not automatically make every system cheaper or safer. Organizations still need to design security, manage access, monitor usage, and control costs.

## 3. Compute, storage, and networking

Applications commonly depend on three foundational resource areas:

- **Compute:** Processing resources that run applications, such as virtual machines and CPUs.
- **Storage:** Resources used to keep files, databases, backups, and other data.
- **Networking:** Connections that let systems communicate with one another and with users.

These resources work together to deliver services to end users.

## 4. On-premises vs. cloud

**On-premises** means an organization runs infrastructure in its own facilities or in a data center it directly manages.

**Cloud** means the organization uses computing services supplied by a cloud provider. The provider operates the underlying infrastructure, while the customer remains responsible for the parts of the service they manage.

| On-premises infrastructure | Cloud services |
|---|---|
| Often requires purchasing physical hardware | Resources can often be rented on demand |
| Organization arranges facilities, power, and cooling | Provider manages the physical data-center facilities |
| Expanding capacity may require hardware purchases | Capacity can often be adjusted more quickly |
| Organization manages much of the physical infrastructure | Management responsibilities are shared with the provider |

## 5. Common business examples

- A business hosts its website on cloud virtual machines.
- A team stores shared documents in an online storage service.
- An application uses a managed cloud database.
- A company increases its computing capacity during a busy sales period.

## Key takeaway

Cloud computing gives organizations access to computing resources without requiring them to own and operate every physical component themselves.
